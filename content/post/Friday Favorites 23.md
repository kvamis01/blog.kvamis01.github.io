Happy Friday,

[Cue the music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bu55q_3YtOY), brew that pot. When did summers get so busy, and how is it already late July! I’m trying to soak in some sunny days, but they’re passing me by.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

1. “Writing is hard because your human brain, the one that’s been running and updating the biological human survival software for roughly 200,000 years, is genuinely convinced that putting words on a page might kill you.” There’s neuroscience behind why [our brain sees writing as quietly dangerous](https://lithub.com/why-writing-feels-so-hard-and-how-to-make-it-easier/) and how meditation can help.
2. [Hot people read books](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/jul/22/reading-books-fashion-brands-literary-clubs), as it turns out. It’s funny how we collectively lament the end of reading thanks to social media and AI, but simultaneously there are so many book clubs and buzz over new subgenres.
3. The [Lo-Fi Cinema](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZmJZ1RcgIc) YouTube channel have the best video essays about the creative process and identity, and their latest about uncertainty and rigidity in your artistic path really hit home for me. It reminds me of a Rumi quote I stumbled upon this morning, serendipitously: “As you start to walk on the way, the way appears.”
4. Everyone’s talking about “The Odyssey,” and I’m waiting until Tuesday to see it at our old three-screen theater (cheap movie night is the best night!), but it has me remembering this [great interview with Emily Wilson](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/emily-wilson/), the first female translator to put The Odyssey and The Iliad into English. (Tyler Cowen quickly jumps between topics with guests, which is jarring at first but youg et used to it).
5. Emily Wilson also knows how to have fun: When we were all quarantining over the summer of 2020, she decided to [read and act out a bit of “The Odyssey” every day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gK1gNQPpJKc&list=PLb79xn1QTw3xKRRUemOrdCU6AXo686dGV&index=4), complete with wigs, sunglasses, and other props she had in her home. Having fun with your work is important!
6. That reminds of of Austin Kleon’s chapter “Don’t Take Things Too Seriously” from his new book “[Don’t Call It Art](https://bookshop.org/p/books/don-t-call-it-art-10-ways-to-create-like-a-kid-again-austin-kleon/3e44c1bf468cd62e)”: find who’s having fun, pretend it’s all a comedy, and play the fool. “Ask yourself frequently, ‘Am I having fun?’ The answer needn’t always be yes. But if it’s always no, it’s time for a new project or a new career,” says Stephen King.
7. I’ve lifted a few pens from restaurants I love as a memento to a good memory (a few restaurants of which have now closed), so this tight essay about [pilfering restaurant ephemera](https://www.theoldghosts.com/stories/the-best-pen-can-only-be-stolen/?utm_source=The%20List%20-%20High%20Engagement%20%28Clicked%20or%20placed%20order%20in%20the%20last%2060%20days%29&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Staple%20Day%2034%20-%20High%20Engagement%20%282026-07-23%29&_kx=8e83pmYhuJztexpKphuOolsikC8PSmmix30ftXiS1j8.Vc5bVr) is very relatable. (I also share a love for Field Notes, Pilot G2s, and abhorring Bic Clic Stics)
8. [Brendan’s latest video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UeUQu2eOHW4) about why we run is really about why we do anything: to keep doing it. (See: writing, reading, lifting, going for walks)
9. Nearly every day, Kent Peterson types up a short, interesting essay or thought on his typewriter, pushes a few hand-carved ink stamps onto the paper, snaps a photo, and uploads it to YouTube — and this recent post “[Lessons in Slowing Down](https://kentpeterson.substack.com/p/lessons-in-slowing-down?utm_source=profile&utm_medium=reader2)” gets at why, I think, and also shows off a particularly old typewriter (he has a big collection!).
10. Finally, a [watch](https://mrjoneswatches.com/products/a-perfectly-useless-afternoon) that tells the time and also shows exactly how I plan to spend my weekend.
