Happy Friday,

[Cue the music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aSbz27tynn8), brew that pot. I took a month off writing and vacationed in Italy and Greece. I drank a bunch of wine, reserved a car on the wrong continent, and loved seeing a bunch of ancient Greek ruins. Now Minnesota is dark and cold — but at least the Great Horned Owls have ousted the Barred Owls from the copse of trees across the street.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

1. We got another [rare Robert Caro interview](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/robert-caro), this time in his office with Dwarkesh Patel asking about his life’s work, reserach practices, and how his last LBJ volume is going. Imagine being complimented on your interview questions by Caro several times! A treat.
2. Speaking of Caro, this week “The Paris Review” unlocked [their 2016 interview with him](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/6442/the-art-of-biography-no-5-robert-caro?utm_source=The+Paris+Review+Newsletter&utm_campaign=40b4c5ab12-TPRMailchimp_202601011_REDUX_+JOYWILLIAMS_COPY_01&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_35491ea532-40b4c5ab12-485212283&mc_cid=40b4c5ab12) from their archive. “There’s no hope of reading it all. You’d need several lifetimes. But you want to try to do as much as possible, because you never know what you will find.”
3. I’ve been reading “[Art & Fear](https://bookshop.org/p/books/art-fear-observations-on-the-perils-and-rewards-of-artmaking-david-bayles/530f9c8b317d9cb2),” which is surfacing up a lot of creative truths I’ve slowly uncovered and/or avoided. One of the first is that art is made by everyday people. Which is exactly what [Brendan Leonard says in this video](https://bookshop.org/p/books/art-fear-observations-on-the-perils-and-rewards-of-artmaking-david-bayles/530f9c8b317d9cb2) about making bad art: “I thought famous people made art, and they were special. But, and nobody told me this, a lot of times it’s the other way around. Regular people make art, and sometimes — not very often — they become famous because of it. Famous art is just one example of art.”
4. I heard about “Art & Fear” years ago via Austin Kleon, my patron saint of creativity, in [this blog post](https://austinkleon.com/2020/12/10/quantity-leads-to-quality-the-origin-of-a-parable/) suggesting beginners focus on their quantity of work before quality, an anecdote I’ve internalized and [wrote about last week](https://blog.isakkvam.com/post/the-ray-bradbury-challenge/).
5. Ray Bradbury shared the same idea in [his keynote address](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_W-r7ABrMYU&t=186s) from 2001: every day, read one poem, one short story, and one essay. Every week, write one short story. At the end of the year, you’ll be full of ideas — and it’s impossible to write 52 short stories and have every one be bad. I tried this challenge at the beginning of this month, but then the Midterms happened — a practice to restart!
6. I plowed through “[Writers & Lovers](https://bookshop.org/p/books/writers-lovers-lily-king/c54f26ea3c4c7392)” by Lily King last weekend. I was so invested in the protagonist, cheering on her working on her novel, unsure of which guy she should date — it [left me wondering](https://blog.isakkvam.com/post/reading-books-in-days-not-weeks/) whether you read good books quickly because they’re good or if reading book quickly can make them seem better. Maybe a chicken or egg scenario.
7. Love [Elizabeth Gilbert’s advice](https://howiwrite.substack.com/p/elizabeth-gilbert-this-is-what-40) to 1) not go into debt for a creative degree that will limit your future opportunities because now you have big bills to pay, and 2) having a bias to go experience life, good and bad, instead of sitting around thinking.
8. Very excited for today’s [Super Deluxe release](https://open.spotify.com/album/2mJUQL7i5kgxHjOAZ1YScA?si=8yfOkakvQx6cI_c1Mn9pFA) of Talking Heads’ “Fear of Music,” my first favorite album of theirs, which includes two extra discs of live performances. Jonathan Lethem wrote a great [_33 1/3_ book about the album](https://bookshop.org/p/books/talking-heads-fear-of-music-jonathan-lethem/d98f3c36894c37aa) I loved.
9. Lethem also wrote “[Motherless Brooklyn](https://bookshop.org/p/books/motherless-brooklyn-jonathan-lethem/085157d196aa452b?ean=9780375724831),” which Edward Norton adapted into a movie that combined combined Caro’s “[The Power Broker](https://bookshop.org/p/books/the-power-broker-robert-moses-and-the-fall-of-new-york-robert-a-caro/d422fb89e9efb810?ean=9780394720241)” storyline — which is how I first got into Caro! His [Tim Ferriss Show podcast interview](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-tim-ferriss-show/id863897795?i=1000455592697) gets into how he combined both books into something totally new.
10. Which is the first piece of advice in Austin Kleon’s “[Steal Like an Artist](https://bookshop.org/p/books/steal-like-an-artist-10-things-nobody-told-you-about-being-creative-austin-kleon/9f9278e5624c9947)” — nothing is original! [He quotes](https://austinkleon.com/tag/influences/) Jonathan Lethem saying that when people call something “original,” nine times out of ten they just don’t know the references or original sources involved. A kind of “scenius” that Kleon references [Brian Eno](https://austinkleon.com/2017/05/12/scenius/) talking about: that great ideas happen not from an individual but groups of people looking at each others’ work, copying each other, and building new things. (To tie together even more: Brian Eno joined Talking Heads to make “Fear of Music,” then pushed the band in a new direction by taking influence from [Fela Kuti](https://blog.isakkvam.com/post/friday-favorites-17/) to make “[Remain In Light](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w6T_X7MXg40&list=PLfimnwaZdumi5_CxCzqG3Mpv1PqHvr2VW)” and one of their best songs, “[Once In A Lifetime](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5IsSpAOD6K8)”)

That’s enough recommendations/connections for one week!
