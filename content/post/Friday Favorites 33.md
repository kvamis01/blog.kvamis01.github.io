Happy Friday,

[Cue the music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lr62Jcse6us), brew that pot. It’s the last nice week of November, I roasted brats over a bonfire last night, and my neighbor’s curious cat Tux wandered over to investigate.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

1. It’s been a while since I read a Murakami novel, but I vividly remember how he would weave in specific classical and jazz music into the scenes. So it’s cool someone put made a [two-hour mix of jazz](https://kottke.org/26/09/two-hour-mix-of-the-jazz-in-haruki-murakami-novels) from his books.
2. Gabe Bullard’s new book “[Against Convenience](https://bookshop.org/p/books/against-convenience-embracing-friction-in-an-age-of-endless-ease-gabe-bullard/1e5ab524a5574880)” seems like a philosophical salve (or challenge?) in modern work culture. I’m sure to enjoy it if it’s similar to [his essay](https://gabebullard.substack.com/p/confessions-of-a-craft-obsessive) about the lessons we learn from forgotten mentors and hand-me-down craft practices.
3. I’m stealing the “Why would you think it’d be different?” line that the mentor says to the writer in “[Art & Fear](https://bookshop.org/p/books/art-fear-observations-on-the-perils-and-rewards-of-artmaking-david-bayles/530f9c8b317d9cb2?ean=9780961454739)” complaining that what he’s writing is so much worse than how he imagines it in his head. A helpful phrase you can use for lots of similar thought patterns!
4. I stopped tracking how many books/pages I read each year because I began focusing too much on quantity (squeeze in _more!_) than quality (getting lost in a book). And while I read less this year than last, I like [this essay from Alan Jacobs](https://blog.ayjay.org/whos-counting/) about not making year-end lists.
5. Every now and then I spend _hours_ Austin Kleon’s blog. This time I read [every one of his annual “what I read this year” posts](https://austinkleon.com/tag/my-reading-year/) and now my “sounds interesting” list of books has ballooned. Too many books to fit in a lifetime — but what a lifetime.
6. [Jack Black’s advice](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dfz0JZsEu80/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&jplx=NTc4MTIwNjQ2YQ==) is to listen to every album from a band chronologically, all the way through, which is exactly what I do a lot while working? Do people not do this? Maybe it’s because I’m an album guy not a shuffle guy. Especially fun for jazz musicians with deep catalogs! Try Miles Davis, John Coltraine, and Sun Ra.
7. You don’t have to be a fan of Slayyyter to enjoy [this article](https://defector.com/i-bought-a-1-vhs-and-became-an-enemy-of-slayyyter-fans?giftLink=fa30e2f3909b8e63d222c970cedca32e&utm_source=defector.beehiiv.com&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=ng-newsletter-11-19&_bhlid=acce58caec29c779fbde648d9cb50a775703dd2e) about a 45-year-old guy who found a mysterious VHS tape at his local secondhand shop, posted about it online, and got a glimpse into a fanbase he’d hardly heard of.
8. I listened to Slayyyter’s “[WORST GIRL IN AMERICA](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLLg3TrvTuhgl-jQwNXcGTwNswKtch0O-a&si=5xkMZKo_ZqYdia70)” a lot during Halloween, but 2026 has been a GREAT year for pop music! I loved the new [Charli xcx](https://youtu.be/K60NXmD89s8?si=b8gUI4Ym1zEWxuYl), [Phoebe Bridgers](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOyCQAKbupsA&si=pzqG72EAVxv4XQ2D), [Olivia Rodrigo](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLTHaRGmXxByI&si=vJZ3ye1FPNdgHJAe), [Gracie Abrams](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLTLEX7UIqKEM&si=z6u62OQPpB4uiwba), and [Remi Wolf](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLPCxJ5iePBWI&si=qQs8nWQ3WmWxfTjK) albums. And I discovered lots of new-to-me bands with new albums, too: [Tiffany Day](https://youtu.be/1Niuu4IevL8?si=8PJ_zUibPTCXXH8X), [ADÉLA](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLYQcL4IeJnzo&si=BPxcIlaGZfXBTgHV), [daine](https://youtu.be/PuQJ4XEKpoU?si=REahb12oVNmyBp7Y), [underscores](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLxA687tYuMWhod27FrrBZqJiFn0mPuO-V&si=Pnzi4cfsSgOeYEW4), and [Sophia Stel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jq5ST6wpIN8&list=RDjq5ST6wpIN8&start_radio=1).
9. Love Keala Kendall’s [idea of a like list](https://lithub.com/how-ten-national-book-award-finalists-tackle-writers-block/), a “running inventory of elements that make me excited about the story” when she’s working on a new writing project.
10. I think most [“get off your phone” articles](https://huckberry.com/journal/posts/30-ways-to-get-off-and-stay-off-your-phone) are unsatisfying because the truest challenge isn’t finding fun offline hobbies but just actually confronting and allowing the discomfort of boredom and no stimulation to exist (#22 in the list) . I stared at the floor for two minutes in the dentist’s waiting room this week and it felt WEIRD. And that’s weird!!
