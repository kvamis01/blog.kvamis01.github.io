Two poems from Wendell Berry

September 3, 2026

RIP Wendell Berry, whose poems, novels, essays, and environmental thinking challenged people to take better care of ourselves, our communities, and the land.

I read one of his books of agrarian essays in college, but what’s stuck with me ever since is his poetry. I first read this one as a sophomore in college, and it has that comfortable, relaxing feeling only poetry can make:

The Peace of the Wild Things
Wendell Berry

When despair for the world grows in me
and I wake in the night at the least sound
in fear of what my life and my children’s lives may be,
I go and lie down where the wood drake
rests in his beauty on the water, and the great heron feeds.
I come into the peace of wild things
who do not tax their lives with forethought
of grief. I come into the presence of still water.
And I feel above me the day-blind stars
waiting with their light, for a time
I rest in the grace of the world, and am free.

One of my favorite Berry essays is “Why I Am not Going To Buy A Computer,” where he shares his 9 rules for new technology. It was written in 1987, but it’s just as relevant today as then. I remember it being a small reason I got into typewriters during the pandemic (the main reason is that they make me really happy, like a big bookshelf of books does).

Wendell didn’t like to write at a computer; he used a pencil or pen and paper, or his wife typed his work on their Royal standard typewriter — which they “bought new in 1956 and as good now as it was then.” My mother-in-law has a great Royal standard (with gravestone keys), but my main typer is my 1964 Smith Corona Galaxie II, which I used to type up a few Berry poems.

Another Berry poem I like is “A Meeting”:

A Meeting
Wendell Berry

In a dream I meet
my dead friend. He has,
I know, gone long and far,
and yet he is the same.
For the dead are changeless.
They grow no older.
It is I who have changed,
grown strange to what I was.
Yet I, the changed one,
ask: ‘How you been?’
He grins and looks at me.
’I been eating peaches
off some mighty fine trees.’
