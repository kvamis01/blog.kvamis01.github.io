Friday Favorites 33

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

November 20, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. It’s the last nice week of November, I roasted brats over a bonfire last night, and my neighbor’s curious cat Tux wandered over to investigate.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. It’s been a while since I read a Murakami novel, but I vividly remember how he would weave in specific classical and jazz music into the scenes. So it’s cool someone put made a two-hour mix of jazz from his books.
  2. Gabe Bullard’s new book “Against Convenience” seems like a philosophical salve (or challenge?) in modern work culture. I’m sure to enjoy it if it’s similar to his essay about the lessons we learn from forgotten mentors and hand-me-down craft practices.
  3. I’m stealing the “Why would you think it’d be different?” line that the mentor says to the writer in “Art & Fear” complaining that what he’s writing is so much worse than how he imagines it in his head. A helpful phrase you can use for lots of similar thought patterns!
  4. I stopped tracking how many books/pages I read each year because I began focusing too much on quantity (squeeze in more!) than quality (getting lost in a book). And while I read less this year than last, I like this essay from Alan Jacobs about not making year-end lists.
  5. Every now and then I spend hours Austin Kleon’s blog. This time I read every one of his annual “what I read this year” posts and now my “sounds interesting” list of books has ballooned. Too many books to fit in a lifetime — but what a lifetime.
  6. Jack Black’s advice is to listen to every album from a band chronologically, all the way through, which is exactly what I do a lot while working? Do people not do this? Maybe it’s because I’m an album guy not a shuffle guy. Especially fun for jazz musicians with deep catalogs! Try Miles Davis, John Coltraine, and Sun Ra.
  7. You don’t have to be a fan of Slayyyter to enjoy this article about a 45-year-old guy who found a mysterious VHS tape at his local secondhand shop, posted about it online, and got a glimpse into a fanbase he’d hardly heard of.2
  8. I listened to Slayyyter’s “WORST GIRL IN AMERICA” a lot during Halloween, but 2026 has been a GREAT year for pop music! I loved the new Charli xcx, Phoebe Bridgers, Olivia Rodrigo, Gracie Abrams, and Remi Wolf albums. And I discovered lots of new-to-me bands with new albums, too: Tiffany Day, ADÉLA, daine, underscores, and Sophia Stel.
  9. Love Keala Kendall’s idea of a like list, a “running inventory of elements that make me excited about the story” when she’s working on a new writing project.
  10. I think most “get off your phone” articles are unsatisfying because the truest challenge isn’t finding fun offline hobbies but just actually confronting and allowing the discomfort of boredom and no stimulation to exist (#22 in the list) . I stared at the floor for two minutes in the dentist’s waiting room this week and it felt WEIRD. And that’s weird!!

See you on down the dusty trail,

Friday Favorites 23

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

July 24, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. When did summers get so busy, and how is it already late July! I’m trying to soak in some sunny days, but they’re passing me by.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. “Writing is hard because your human brain, the one that’s been running and updating the biological human survival software for roughly 200,000 years, is genuinely convinced that putting words on a page might kill you.” There’s neuroscience behind why our brain sees writing as quietly dangerous and how meditation can help.
  2. Hot people read books, as it turns out. It’s funny how we collectively lament the end of reading thanks to social media and AI, but simultaneously there are so many book clubs and buzz over new subgenres.
  3. The Lo-Fi Cinema YouTube channel have the best video essays about the creative process and identity, and their latest about uncertainty and rigidity in your artistic path really hit home for me. It reminds me of a Rumi quote I stumbled upon this morning, serendipitously: “As you start to walk on the way, the way appears.”
  4. Everyone’s talking about “The Odyssey,” and I’m waiting until Tuesday to see it at our old three-screen theater (cheap movie night is the best night!), but it has me remembering this great interview with Emily Wilson, the first female translator to put The Odyssey and The Iliad into English. (Tyler Cowen quickly jumps between topics with guests, which is jarring at first but youg et used to it).
  5. Emily Wilson also knows how to have fun: When we were all quarantining over the summer of 2020, she decided to read and act out a bit of “The Odyssey” every day, complete with wigs, sunglasses, and other props she had in her home. Having fun with your work is important!
  6. That reminds of of Austin Kleon’s chapter “Don’t Take Things Too Seriously” from his new book “Don’t Call It Art”: find who’s having fun, pretend it’s all a comedy, and play the fool. “Ask yourself frequently, ‘Am I having fun?’ The answer needn’t always be yes. But if it’s always no, it’s time for a new project or a new career,” says Stephen King.
  7. I’ve lifted a few pens from restaurants I love as a memento to a good memory (a few restaurants of which have now closed), so this tight essay about pilfering restaurant ephemera is very relatable. (I also share a love for Field Notes, Pilot G2s, and abhorring Bic Clic Stics)
  8. Brendan’s latest video about why we run is really about why we do anything: to keep doing it. (See: writing, reading, lifting, going for walks)
  9. Nearly every day, Kent Peterson types up a short, interesting essay or thought on his typewriter, pushes a few hand-carved ink stamps onto the paper, snaps a photo, and uploads it to YouTube — and this recent post “Lessons in Slowing Down” gets at why, I think, and also shows off a particularly old typewriter (he has a big collection!).
  10. Finally, a watch that tells the time and also shows exactly how I plan to spend my weekend.

Friday Favorites 17

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

June 12, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. I spent my evenings this week pressure-washing my concrete and listening to Stephen King narrate his book “Bag of Bones.” Recommend!

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. Really enjoying Austin Kleon’s new book “Don’t Call It Art” and was delighted to find a short sub-chapter about having fun — which pairs with a question I’ve been asking myself this spring: What if this were fun?
  2. I’m always fascinated by how writers spent their days before the internet (both the constant procrastination and the endless barrage of information), so this article with Louis L’Amour’s typewritten weekly to-do lists (nevermind the title) was so fun to read. It feels simultaneously so similar and yet dissimilar to modern routines.
  3. Isn’t it fun to learn a new word for something you already do? I decided to blog more often this year about what I’m reading, thinking, and doing, and turns out that’s a “digital garden” of exploring and connecting ideas.
  4. There’s a plethora of “the internet has made it harder to read” articles, but I found this article interesting because it wondered if having a job where you read bad writing (emails, reports, social media, etc.) all day makes it harder to wind down with a good book.
  5. Meanwhile, here’s a guy who reads good writing every day for his job: “Not every executive can read every book,” he explains. “Everybody’s busy and they don’t have time to read everything.” Clark Speicher works for Hollywood reads a book every day or two (especially Lit), surgically breaks it down beat by beat, and recommends how it be adapted.
  6. Every since I started percussion in band in fifth grade, I’ve been tapping on surfaces around me. So I decided to level up and learn the sticking for a tough song: the drum intro for Van Halen’s Hot for Teacher. There’s something satisfying about working through how a song works, it feels similar to doing a Rubik’s Cube in my opinion.
  7. There is SO much energy in this Die Spitz show on KEXP, this is a blast.
  8. Fela Kuti was a big inspiration for David Byrne and Brian Eno for Talking Heads’ “Remain in Light” album, but I didn’t know Hanif Abduraqib was a creative producer for the recent podcast mini-series “Fela Kuti: Fear No Man.” Plus Jad Abumrad from Radiolab!
  9. Love this Instagram account from Nicholas Triolo sharing 200 books about ecology and justice in 200 words or less.
  10. With summer heating up, now’s a perfect time to watch an old movie that pairs with higher temps.

What if this were fun?

June 9, 2026

In the 2010s, I heard Tim Ferriss share a phrase that guided his work: what would this look like if this were easy?

A question I’ve been asking myself this spring is: What if this were fun?

Most of my best work has come from doing something fun. It’s the rule of cool! Having fun is fun, and fun is contagious. You can tell when a writer is bored by their subject and putting no effort into making it more engaging or readable. You can also tell when a writer is having fun, like Bill Bryson or Hunter S. Thompson or Charles Portis.
