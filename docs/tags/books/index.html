Friday Favorites 33

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

November 20, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. It’s the last nice week of November, I roasted brats over a bonfire last night, and my neighbor’s curious cat Tux wandered over to investigate.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. It’s been a while since I read a Murakami novel, but I vividly remember how he would weave in specific classical and jazz music into the scenes. So it’s cool someone put made a two-hour mix of jazz from his books.
  2. Gabe Bullard’s new book “Against Convenience” seems like a philosophical salve (or challenge?) in modern work culture. I’m sure to enjoy it if it’s similar to his essay about the lessons we learn from forgotten mentors and hand-me-down craft practices.
  3. I’m stealing the “Why would you think it’d be different?” line that the mentor says to the writer in “Art & Fear” complaining that what he’s writing is so much worse than how he imagines it in his head. A helpful phrase you can use for lots of similar thought patterns!
  4. I stopped tracking how many books/pages I read each year because I began focusing too much on quantity (squeeze in more!) than quality (getting lost in a book). And while I read less this year than last, I like this essay from Alan Jacobs about not making year-end lists.
  5. Every now and then I spend hours Austin Kleon’s blog. This time I read every one of his annual “what I read this year” posts and now my “sounds interesting” list of books has ballooned. Too many books to fit in a lifetime — but what a lifetime.
  6. Jack Black’s advice is to listen to every album from a band chronologically, all the way through, which is exactly what I do a lot while working? Do people not do this? Maybe it’s because I’m an album guy not a shuffle guy. Especially fun for jazz musicians with deep catalogs! Try Miles Davis, John Coltraine, and Sun Ra.
  7. You don’t have to be a fan of Slayyyter to enjoy this article about a 45-year-old guy who found a mysterious VHS tape at his local secondhand shop, posted about it online, and got a glimpse into a fanbase he’d hardly heard of.
  8. I listened to Slayyyter’s “WORST GIRL IN AMERICA” a lot during Halloween, but 2026 has been a GREAT year for pop music! I loved the new Charli xcx, Phoebe Bridgers, Olivia Rodrigo, Gracie Abrams, and Remi Wolf albums. And I discovered lots of new-to-me bands with new albums, too: Tiffany Day, ADÉLA, daine, underscores, and Sophia Stel.
  9. Love Keala Kendall’s idea of a like list, a “running inventory of elements that make me excited about the story” when she’s working on a new writing project.
  10. I think most “get off your phone” articles are unsatisfying because the truest challenge isn’t finding fun offline hobbies but just actually confronting and allowing the discomfort of boredom and no stimulation to exist (#22 in the list) . I stared at the floor for two minutes in the dentist’s waiting room this week and it felt WEIRD. And that’s weird!!

See you on down the dusty trail,

Friday Favorites 21

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

July 10, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. It’s been a long, hot week and I could use a lake.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. This New Yorker owns so many books in his studio apartment, his landlord is trying to evict him. (Goals?)
  2. “I always loved this slogan used by the New York State Lotto: ‘Hey, you never know.’ As someone who’s taught writing for many years, I don’t know a better mantra for anyone involved in the arts.” This George Saunders article has so many good nuggets in it. Another: “Someone might have to live a long time before the thing they were born to say becomes fully formed and accessible to them.”
  3. You can now browse every Criterion Closet pick in a virtual closet on this cool web app.
  4. Today is the last day to bid on the American Library Association’s iconic READ posters of celebrities holding their favorite books. (Of course Nicolas Cage’s is Siddhartha.)
  5. I like when people a) make arbitrary stupid goals, and 2) use them as an interesting hook for doing something, so I’m still thinking about how Beau Miles ran a marathon from home by running one mile around his house every hour for 24 hours straight and fitting in as many chores as possible in-between.
  6. I wrote about Henry David Thoreau’s Type II Fun trip camping in the woods, foraging for food, and using some old lumber as a sleeping bag to stay warm at night, which reminds me of this old Dirtbag Diaries podcast episode about the three types of outdoor fun.
  7. How I work isn’t all that different from a Large Language Model: I read a bunch, let the ideas simmer, and then draw on them all — consciously or not — to write. The problem is that now a bunch of stuff I read is AI-generated whether I know it or not, which is affecting the whole process like a genetic mutation.
  8. I’ve been missing the fried bologna sandwiches and classic country bands of Robert’s in Nashville from this time of year last year, so I’ve been listening to this Vintage Country playlist on Spotify a bunch this week.
  9. My Stephen King kick continues with “Different Seasons,” a collection of novellas including “The Body” — which is probably better known for its adaptation into the movie “Stand By Me.” I loved this movie when I was younger: a group of boys hiking, telling stories, getting into mischief and adventure — and I’m delighted how well it was adapted and sad it was banned in Utah schools this week. (I guess they also banned “Bag of Bones” which I also just finished?)
  10. I like this idea of using a typewriter to write a cover of an essay/book you like, like a shorter version you make for yourself.
