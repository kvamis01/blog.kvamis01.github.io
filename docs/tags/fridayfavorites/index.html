Friday Favorites 28

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

August 28, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. The Minnesota Fair officially kicked off, marking the end of summer, and I can’t wait to eat every pickle-related item.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. RIP Dolly Parton, RIP Tim Curry; two phenomenal human beings who made the world a brighter place. A nugget of wisdom I read about them both this week: take your cause seriously, but don’t take yourself too seriously. Help others, but don’t let your ego get in the way. Now it’s time to watch “9 to 5” and “Clue.”
  2. Forget reading in season, I’m reading out of season and spending the end of summer reading about a bunch of sailors whose ship froze stuck to the pack ice during an arctic winter while a mysterious creature stalks them.
  3. At night while I read “The Terror” I’ve been listening to this dark 2007 ambient album “Erebus & Terror” by David Bickley inspired by Sir John Franklin’s doomed expedition in the 1840s. I like it more than the official soundtrack to the miniseries of “The Terror.”
  4. “The Terror” was included in a discontinued “Winter reads” series from The Guardian, which has 20 other wintry book recommendations I might dip into before the temps cool down.
  5. Brendan gets it.
  6. Loved hearing Adrian Tchaikovsky’s approach to good writing — mostly by starting with worldbuilding to create realistic characters, allowing the plot to unfold from there.
  7. In extremely good news for me and my coffee habit, the American Heart Association released a review of many studies found that most adults can have up to 5 cups of coffee a day without adverse effects, and 2 to 4 cups can be good for you.
  8. So excited for the upcoming concert film of David Byrne’s “Who Is the Sky?” tour. I missed his show when he came to Minneapolis, but we came through when his violinist’s five-string instrument
  9. “It’s a bit like making dough: the resting is the most important part of the recipe. You cannot skip it or rush.” Lofi Cinema on giving your creative ideas time to rest.
  10. Coming back to this George Saunders letter, where he writes “what if we tried to believe that everything we did and felt (and avoided, and screwed up) was, not only part of our long artistic journey, but was the journey?” Which reminds me of Anne Lamott saying it’s not that writers need to have really interesting experiences to tell good stories, but that they have to actually observe — to really notice and be aware of their experiences — to tell good stories.

Friday Favorites 27

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

August 21, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. The humidity’s finally left Minnesota, and I’m so excited to wear pants, listen to new Phoebe Bridgers, and enjoy the end of summer with pizza and tequila old fashioneds.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. I love staring at people’s bookshelves, so this list of David Bryne’s books (which he turned into a public lending library at a 2015 art festival) was fun to read through. Unsurprisingly, lots of music books I’d never heard of.
  2. The “How I Write” podcast has so, so many interesting writers on the show, and the Anne Lamott episode was packed with great advice on writing and living. I especially liked her note on using a thesaurus: “You have to amass a kind of battered old toolbox if you’re going to get anywhere with writing.”
  3. I finally saw the Odyssey last week, sneaking out on a Friday afternoon with a huge bucket of popcorn. The movie’s based on Emily Wilson’s translation (I liked both “The Odyssey” and “The Iliad” from her! More fun than Fitzgerald), and her new book out next month sounds great.
  4. I’ve been reading about the Transcendentalists this year — it started with Robert D. Richardson’s “First We Read, Then We Write,” then Bruce Nichols’ new “The Emerson Circle,” and now I’m reading Laura Dassow Walls’ Thoreau biography, and I’m giggling at the story of that time Thoreau started a wildfire and felt bad about it for years and years.
  5. “The cost of a thing is the amount of what I will call life which is required to be exchanged for it, immediately or in the long run.” Thoreau’s very popular amongst young adult Chinese readers. I read Walden in my freshman-year J-term course in college, a wonderful one-month, one-class “mini-semester” that I usually filled with books and coffee.
  6. Phoebe Bridgers has a new album out, and I’m a sucker for the vocoder on the very first track “The Outside.” I first loved vocoder from Imogen Heap’s “Hide and Seek,” but fell in love with the sound in Bon Iver’s vocoder-adjacent “715 - CR∑∑KS,” and more recently Lorde’s “Clearblue.”
  7. As much as I yearn for deeply experiencing each season as it comes and goes, it seems I like to read out-of-season — because I started reading Dan Simmons’ “The Terror” last night after a Barnes & Noble employee recommended it to my “I’ve been reading too much Stephen King” query, and I was immediately transported to the cold, frozen north pole when an 1840s steamship (same decade Thoreau started his wildfire!) is trapped — and haunted — by something sinister on the ice.
  8. Loved this person reading “The Count of Monte Cristo” in one sitting (21 hours). I don’t think I have the attention span for marathon-reading, but this one for “The Power Broker” this fall could change my mind.
  9. As if I needed another reason to read more Le Guin. (I just got her “Steering the Craft” this month and am excited - and intimidated - to work through it.)
  10. Been following Ed Roberson’s Mountain and Prairie podcast for years. Very excited he’s putting together a book club for folks who love public lands and the American West.

Friday Favorites 26

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

August 14, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. This week I learned my plumber plays the hurdy-gurdy at the Rennaissance Festival, so I guess that’s as good a reason as any to go for the first time?

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. I listened to this interview with Maria Popova of “The Marginalian,” one of the few blogs I’ve followed for over a decade — and I eventually realized I needed to pull it up and transcribe the entire thing for myself: her lack of productivity, no editorial calendars, why archives are deeper than the internet, walking four hours a day to think (making her writing merely transcribing) — there’s just so much packed into this interview.
  2. I’m on a roll, because I also listened to Michael Pollan on the same podcast talk about how writing is like a compost heap, laundry lines as narrative, why what we read before bed matters, and so much more. I loved that Pollan’s influenced by Hoaglund’s nature writing — I read his essay collection “On Nature” in 2014 and it had a big impact on me.
  3. This letter on nostalgia reminds me of that line from Patricia Lockwood: “You’ll be nostalgic for this, too, if you make it.”
  4. My favorite niche, hyper-specific thing biographies do is when they go way, way back to explore how an area’s geologic history affected the person they’re writing about.
  5. If you’re a writer, maybe you can relate to this short article about the importance of deadlines? This Friday Favorites post would never make it without a weekly, firm deadline holding me accountable (my Tuesday blog post got deferred to today, in no small part because the deadline was less firm!).
  6. This 1:43-minute video of all the weird sounds David Brynes makes in the Talking Heads discography is hilarious and something I’ve long wanted to create myself.
  7. Really relate to Carmen Maria Machado in how her writing happens in short, concentrated bursts. As alluring as a disciplined writing practice is, it’s always a fantasy to me!
  8. That said, a lot of authors know to show up even when they don’t feel like it.
  9. One Main Character of the Day on BlueSky this week was a writer at Axios (the place that writes news for short attention spans) who described how he uses AI to help him read books his attention span can’t handle. But more interesting is this Defector article responding to it about why we read.
  10. If you like books and maps, boy do I have a website for you.

Friday Favorites 25

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

August 7, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. I’ve been listening to Charli xcx all week and soaking up the dog days of summer before it’s pants season.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. A few years ago I watched “Adaptation” and became really interested in how Hollywood and writers can use “The Hero’s Journey” to structure stories, which led me to read Vogler’s “The Writer’s Journey” and Joseph Campbell’s “The Hero with a Thousand Faces.” So I really enjoyed this 90-minute interview with Steven Pressfield on “How I Write”, who talks a lot about structure, Hollywood, writing good stories, and why we like The Hero’s Journey so much.
  2. I’m still listening to nothing but “No One Lasts Forever.” Voice samples in music are nothing new, but it is interesting how the speech-to-song illusion can make certain statements feel so musical.
  3. I wrote about my love for vintage mass market paperbacks — those gorgeous, portable books I recall from 60s and 70s lit classics and 80s horror — and why I think they’re the best book format.
  4. This Tuesday was August 4, 2026 — the day of Ray Bradbury’s 1950 short story “There Will Come Soft Rains,” which makes it as good a time to read it as any.
  5. I have a few meals I’ve made several times a week for years (popcorn, bean bowls) — and beans are a hot food item this year — so I’m thinking about that time Beau Miles ate nothing but canned beans for 40 days then ran 50 kilometers. (If I did this I would consider coffee a form of bean and have a better adjustment.)
  6. Every couple years a local news station will talk about why people still like typewriters, and I’ll watch them every time. This one’s about Gramercy in New York City, the same store Ruth Ozeki got hers.
  7. “I can’t dance, I’ve got two left feet!” “I thought he was kidding.” “But I wasn’t, I was born with two left feet.” “Best In Show” is a really funny movie, and after talking with a few people older than me, a once-popular movie that’s not talked about much anymore!
  8. Sorry, this newsletter is becoming a place I share everything I see about typewriters, but Andrew Loura’s restoration of his family’s 1919 Underwood was very satisfying.
  9. I finished Stephen King’s “Duma Key” about a painter grieving a life-altering event who moves to a vacation community and slowly uncovers its dark past as it impacts their creative process. Which is exactly the same premise as “Bag of Bones,” which I read earlier this summer. I love when writers return to the same well to explore the same idea in new ways.
  10. I relate to a lot in this video essay about ordinariness feeling boring, being stuck in the past or future, and overthinking instead of experiencing your life.

Friday Favorites 24

10 interesting things I'm recommending this week

July 31, 2026

Happy Friday,

Cue the music, brew that pot. I’m sending this newsletter out before killing time at my local bookstore before catching a matinee of “The Odyssey” — an ideal way to start the weekend early.

Here are 10 interesting things worth sharing this week:

  1. I’ve been listening to Charli xcx’s “No One Lasts Forever” on repeat since Tuesday, the one with filmmaker David Cronenberg talking about death, mortality, and art. Goosebumps. So I loved this deep dive into the song with Charli, AG Cooke, and Finn Keane. I assumed they sampled an interview, but Charli personally recorded him! It reminded me of Steve Reich’s “It’s Gonna Rain” and “Different Trains,” and it was neat to hear AG Cooke confirm it.
  2. Seriously impressed by this person’s dedication to building a typewriter-inspired writerdeck, complete with 3-D printing a typewriter shell, creating an LED word counter, and a sliding screen. Puts my writerdeck (ViWoods e-reader plus a clacky keyboard) to shame!
  3. I don’t take for granted how great music streaming is — you can listen to anything, whenever you want! A miracle. But there’s also too much to choose from, so I’ve been enjoying curated radio shows more: American Routes, David Byrne Radio, Mark Hoppus’ After Hours Radio, and the weekly Bob Dylan radio hour on 103.3.
  4. I might have to find the 37-volume set of Pliny writing about the natural world in the first century A.D. to dip in and out of like Valeria Luiselli suggests. Same but different: I snagged a copy of NYRB’s “The Journal” from Thoreau, a collection of entries about the natural world — its secrets and experiences.
  5. One of the best openers I’ve read in a while: “Take capelin, a small silvery fish in the smelt family. Now add salt. Then throw those two ingredients in 110 vats, each of them about 10 feet tall, and let them sit in a defunct factory for over two decades. The resulting stench might haunt an entire community, as it has the people of St. Mary’s, a coastal town of about 300 people in the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador.”
  6. I’ve been enjoying ending my day by reading a snippet of this New Yorker essay about an international scandal and “Alpine Divorce” — a bonkers story for outdoorsy folks.
  7. “But there is a value, too, in language that dissolves like an afternoon in July, the last rays guttering in the branches as you close your book and go inside.” Great writing about the “Beach Reads” here — but I’d be lying if I didn’t say summer and winter are for big books! Something about the long seasons around the solstice make me want a long story, not an episodic one.
  8. In a huge loss for Minnesotans and people with good taste, Pearson’s — of Salted Nut Roll fame — is closing in Minnesota. I await an in-depth, 3,000-word tribute to the snack and will report back.
  9. I’m more of a “scribble it in a field notes notebook” type of guy but I very much love the appeal of creating a digital garden to have some semblance of organizing what I read online.
  10. And finally, 30 seconds of David Lynch to end your week: “Stay alert, do your work, don’t worry about the world going by; it doesn’t mean you can sit around and not do anything, you’ve got to get your butt in gear and do it.”
